# Question 2 — Human Transcriber Quality Evaluation

## 1. Executive Summary
This report outlines a robust, data-driven methodology to evaluate transcriber quality and detect poor performance on the Josh Talks platform. We propose a multi-signal risk framework designed to automatically detect rushing and minimal effort without penalizing legitimate, efficient transcribers.

## 2. Problem Understanding
Human transcribers review and correct initial transcriptions generated by Whisper AI. Poor-quality transcribers rush tasks without listening to the audio or making necessary edits, degrading dataset quality (Word Error Rate) and wasting resources. Data signals must be used to detect this behavior objectively.

## 3. Dataset Specification
Expected fields: `user_id`, `recording_url`, `whisper_text`, `user_text`, `is_edited`, `duration`, `time_taken_by_user`, `segment_character_per_second`.

## 5. Proposed Feature Engineering
Key metrics to calculate:
- **Task Level:** `time_to_duration_ratio`, `edit_flag`.
- **User Level:** `median_time_duration_ratio`, `median_cps`, `edit_rate`, `task_count`.

## 6. Warning Sign 1: Time-to-Duration Ratio
### What it tells us
Extremely low time relative to audio duration indicates the transcriber isn't listening to the full clip.
### How it is measured
`time_taken_by_user / duration`
### Threshold
*Illustrative:* Bottom 5th percentile of the dataset distribution. Requires actual data calibration.
### False alarms
Perfect Whisper transcriptions, simple audio, or highly experienced transcribers.

## 7. Warning Sign 2: High CPS + Low Editing
### What it tells us
Rapid submission of unedited text suggests copy-through behavior without genuine review.
### How it is measured
User's `median_cps` combined with their overall `edit_rate`.
### Threshold
*Illustrative:* Top 5th percentile for CPS intersecting with bottom 5th percentile for edit rate.
### False alarms
Naturally fast typists encountering highly accurate AI transcriptions.

## 8. Additional Supporting Signal
Consistently high task volume combined with the above warning signs highlights persistent malicious behavior rather than a temporary anomaly.

## 9. User-Level Analysis
Data must be aggregated to the user level using robust statistics (medians over means) to prevent extreme outliers from distorting risk scores.

## 10. Part II — Automated Detection System
A multi-tiered system (GREEN, YELLOW, ORANGE, RED) ensures proportionate responses to suspicious signals, moving away from binary block/allow rules.

## 11. Blocking / Restriction Logic
"If a transcriber repeatedly exhibits low time-to-duration ratios AND high CPS with low edits across a sufficiently large task sample, classify them as high risk and trigger enhanced QC/manual review."

## 12. False Positive Protection
Accounts are never blocked on a single signal (e.g., "fast task"). Rules demand persistence across a minimum sample size and require multi-signal confirmation.

## 13. Engineering Recommendations
Deploy the logic as an asynchronous risk-scoring job that evaluates transcribers over a rolling 7-day window, updating risk tiers automatically.

## 14. Human Review Safeguards
Require manual spot-checks before applying a permanent ban to RED-tiered accounts to handle edge cases.

## 15. Calibration Plan
Upon receipt of the dataset: Validate missing values, compute base metrics, plot distributions (percentiles, IQR), and identify the empirical thresholds that isolate the extreme anomalies.

## 16. Limitations
The primary limitation is the lack of the physical dataset, meaning exact numerical thresholds cannot yet be defined.

## 17. Conclusion & Reflection
- **Why these signals?** They directly measure effort (time spent) and outcome (edits made).
- **Why useful?** They are mathematically objective and difficult for bad actors to fake without actually doing the work.
- **Validation:** Real data is needed to plot distributions and set accurate percentile cutoffs.
- **Improvement:** With more data, we could normalize metrics by language, task type, or audio complexity.
